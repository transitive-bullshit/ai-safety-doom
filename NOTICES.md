# Asset and attribution notes

P(DOOM) is an independent, affectionate parody of AI research culture. Public figures appear as fictional caricatures; their inclusion does not imply participation or endorsement.

The application uses Three.js for rendering and contains an original shooter simulation and authored level. Its current environment, opening composition, and mood draw on Doom 64's Staging Area, including a raised arrival, lower chamber, changes in elevation, dark industrial panels, and restrained sector colors. This is an approximation: it does not reproduce the exact Doom 64 map, physics, weapon rules, or enemy logic.

The application does not bundle a Doom executable, Doom source code, WAD files, or original map data. It includes four original Doom monster death recordings, identified below. Doom and Doom 64 are references to the original games, including the work of id Software and Midway. This project is not affiliated with or endorsed by their creators. The [Doom 64 research note](docs/research/doom64-staging-area.md) links the inspected game capture and automap, original instruction booklet, first-hand level-designer interview, and composer release used to inform this direction.

Production raster artwork in `public/game/` was generated for this project using OpenAI image generation. The prompts, reference usage, and output provenance are recorded in [production-art-prompts.md](docs/design/production-art-prompts.md) and, for the `d64-*` environment textures, [doom64-staging-area.md](docs/research/doom64-staging-area.md). The new textures are generated interpretations, not extracted Doom 64 assets. Concept boards in `docs/design/concepts/` are design references, not screenshots of implemented gameplay. Weapon, creature, and portrait artwork is approximate parody art rather than a claim of exact reproduction.

The four six-pose enemy atlases, their prompts, reference usage, measured frame boundaries, and output provenance are documented in [polish-art.md](docs/design/polish-art.md). They are newly generated parody artwork, not sprites extracted from an existing game.

The subsequent paperclip maximizer, larger Sam server-mech likeness, and four-state Eliezer-inspired HUD portrait were generated with built-in ImageGen. Their final prompts, original files, measured sprite crops, and alpha checks are documented in [character-readability-art.md](docs/design/character-readability-art.md). The earlier assets remain preserved.

The replacement P(DOOM) stone logo and skull menu cursor were generated with built-in ImageGen. Original files, final prompts, and transparency checks are recorded in [title-art.md](docs/design/title-art.md). They are original parody assets; the Nintendo 64 reference does not imply affiliation.

Menu lettering uses Goldman Bold by Jaikishan Patel / The Goldman Project Authors, with a procedural bitmap bevel and surface texture. The bundled font is covered by the [SIL Open Font License 1.1](public/fonts/OFL-Goldman.txt). Font and original-menu sources are documented in [title-menu-reference.md](docs/design/title-menu-reference.md).

The RLHF shotgun uses `public/game/audio/rlhf-shotgun.wav`, a processed combination of [“20 gauge shotgun gunshot” by michorvath](https://freesound.org/people/michorvath/sounds/427595/) and [“shotguncock.wav” by SpringySpringo](https://opengameart.org/content/gun-reload-sounds). Both source recordings are published under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). The fire recording was trimmed, lowered in pitch, equalized, and limited; the pump was trimmed, accelerated, equalized, and mixed into the shot. Original downloaded files, source links, and hashes are preserved in [the audio asset notes](public/game/audio/README.md).

The System Prompt pistol uses `public/game/audio/system-prompt-pistol.wav`, a separate sound-design adaptation of michorvath's same CC0 [20-gauge shotgun recording](https://freesound.org/people/michorvath/sounds/427595/). Its short report was trimmed, slightly raised in pitch, equalized, compressed, softly saturated, limited, and faded. This is an adapted shotgun impulse used for a fictional pistol, not a recording of a real pistol. No pump audio is included. Source and output hashes, signal measurements, and the processing recipe are in [the pistol asset notes](public/game/audio/README.md#system-prompt-pistol-report).

Player pain and death vocals use recordings by **HaelDB** from [“Male Grunt/Yelling sounds”](https://opengameart.org/content/male-gruntyelling-sounds), under the creator's offered [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) option. The files `player-pain-1.wav`, `player-pain-2.wav`, and `player-death.wav` are trimmed, equalized, compressed, and limited excerpts from three performer-3 takes. The longer death take has a slight pitch-preserving tempo adjustment. These are independent recorded human vocals; they do not reproduce or clone a public figure's voice. Original selected WAVs, archive provenance, hashes, and the processing recipe are preserved in [the audio asset notes](public/game/audio/README.md).

Enemy deaths use four original **Doom** recordings from id Software's shareware game data: imp (`DSBGDTH1`), possessed zombie (`DSPODTH1`), demon (`DSSGTDTH`), and Baron of Hell (`DSBRSDTH`). They are stored as `public/game/audio/doom-*-death.wav`, with their native 11,025 Hz mono 8-bit PCM preserved after removing the DMX sound format's edge padding. These game recordings remain copyrighted material of their respective rights holders; they are not CC0 assets and are not covered by this repository's MIT license or the Doom engine source-code license. The [audio asset notes](public/game/audio/README.md#original-doom-monster-death-recordings) record the exact shareware WAD source, checksums, monster mappings, and extraction procedure. No Doom 64 audio is bundled.

Remaining sound effects, the dark ambient soundscape, and the industrial score are synthesized by the original code in `lib/game/audio.ts` and `lib/game/menu-audio.ts`. Low beating tones, filtered air, sparse metallic events, and short stereo room reflections support the selected mood. The score does not reproduce Aubrey Hodges's music. The composer source informing the mood is linked in the [research note](docs/research/doom64-staging-area.md). Procedural signs, pickup illustrations, terminal displays, barrel labels, and remaining panel/door textures are drawn by project code.

Third-party dependencies retain their own licenses, including Three.js (MIT), React (MIT), and Next.js (MIT). Their license files are distributed with their packages. The repository’s application code is covered by its [MIT license](license).
